### Card-Based UI System: Professional Design Specification \#\#\#\#\# 1\. Definition and Philosophical Foundation In the modern landscape of cognitive saturation, "Container-style Design" has shifted from a trend to a structural necessity. Cards function as the atomized units of a digital interface, serving as singular units of thought that encapsulate a unified theme. By isolating data into discrete, high-contrast modules, we manage the primary bottleneck of software performance: human attention. This mental model transforms an undifferentiated stream of data into a manageable collection of digestible segments, ensuring that users can parse information architectures with minimal cognitive friction. The transition from "text on a screen" to an "interactable object" relies on the real-world metaphor of physicality. A card is not a flat graphic; it is a tactile entity. This physicality triggers specific user expectations regarding interaction—cards are objects that can be moved, flipped, or dismissed via swiping. By mirroring the physics of a physical credential, we leverage intuitive understanding of depth and movement, making interfaces feel "thumb-friendly." Architectural choices in card systems are fundamentally driven by the "Authority vs. Reach" philosophical divide, exemplified by the Apple Wallet and Google Wallet paradigms: | Aspect | Authority Model (Apple Wallet) | Reach Model (Google Wallet) | | \------ | \------ | \------ | | **Philosophical Basis** | **Credential Vault:** A sacred zone for trusted, standalone objects. | **Integration Surface:** A data-driven extension of the backend ecosystem. | | **Core Objective** | Predictability \= Trust | Correctness \= Trust | | **Layout Control** | Strict OS-enforced rules; Fixed field positions. | Flexible, device-adaptive templates. | | **Surfacing Behavior** | **Proactive:** Surfaces on Lock Screen via time/location triggers. | **Notification-driven:** Relies on ecosystem integration (Maps/Search). | | **Trust Foundation** | Visual consistency: "See one, know all." | Functional reliability: "It scans correctly at checkout." | These foundational paradigms dictate how we prioritize visual hierarchy and semantic density to support efficient information processing. \#\#\#\#\# 2\. Core Principles of Card Design Strategic design requires a proactive approach to cognitive load management. A card system acts as a visual filter, neutralizing "extraneous load"—the mental effort wasted on poorly organized data. By optimizing for the user's "cognitive budget," we prevent the fatigue that leads to churn. To ensure high-performance processing, all card components must adhere to the following psychological mandates: \* **Information Chunking (Miller’s Law):** We mandate the "chunking" of information to respect the human working memory limit of roughly seven items. Grouping related metrics into a single container allows users to process the card as one concept rather than a series of disparate data points. \* **Visual Grouping (Gestalt Principles):** Use proximity and similarity to signal relationships. By aligning related actions and data points, we make the relationship between elements immediate, reducing the mental energy required to parse the layout. \* **Decision Architecture (Hick’s Law):** Decision-making time increases with the number of choices. We limit the choices within a single card to a primary action area or a tightly defined set of icon buttons to accelerate user throughput. \* **Interaction Physics (Fitts’s Law):** Interaction speed is a function of target size and distance. Cards SHALL maximize their clickable surface area to serve as large touch targets, significantly reducing the "pointing time" and error rate compared to traditional text links. These principles necessitate a rigorous structural policy where every pixel must justify its existence in the hierarchy. \#\#\#\#\# 3\. Card Structure and Anatomy A card functions as a policy engine; the standardized container is the only mandatory element, and all subsequent content must follow a strict semantic hierarchy. We utilize Material Design 3 (MD3) variants to communicate priority: **Elevated** (shadowed for high separation), **Filled** (subtle separation), or **Outlined** (visual boundary for strong emphasis). Hierarchy SHALL be established through typography scaling and whitespace, never through decorative borders. This maximizes "Context Recovery"—the speed at which a user can return to their task after a distraction. \#\#\#\#\#\# Semantic Density and Technical Constraints \* **Character Limits:** To prevent truncation and maintain scannability, we enforce Apple Wallet standards: Primary Fields (\~20 characters), Secondary Fields (\~15), and Auxiliary Fields (\~12). \* **Logo Geometry:** \* **Google Wallet:** Requires square logos at **660x660px** . A 15% content-free safety margin is mandatory to account for Google's automatic circular masking. \* **Apple Wallet:** Supports rectangular logos up to **730x150px** . \* **Text on Media:** Placing text directly on images is forbidden. If a design requires an overlay, a translucent scrim or bounding shape MUST be utilized to ensure the text meets accessibility contrast ratios. \#\#\#\#\# 4\. Layout Usage Patterns Grid-based layouts are the backbone of high-density dashboards, where spatial arrangement dictates the perception of priority. In standard desktop grids, the most critical "Health" or "Status" indicators MUST occupy the top-left quadrant to align with natural scanning patterns. \#\#\#\#\#\# Standard PatternFly Card Types: \* **Aggregate Status:** Reports total counts and flags objects requiring attention (errors/maintenance). \* **Trend:** Displays metric changes over time using sparkline charts. \* **Utilization:** Uses bar or donut charts to report proportional values (percentage of whole). \* **Details:** Displays attribute-value pairs in a two-column description list format. \* **Events:** A chronological, filterable list of system alerts or tasks. \#\#\#\#\#\# Adaptive Grid Policy: Layouts must scale from expanded desktop columns to compact mobile viewports. On **Apple Watch** , designers must account for the fact that **Strip and Thumbnail images are NOT displayed** ; critical information must be accessible via primary text fields. \* **Sizing Strategy:** Cards SHALL be categorized as **Small** (static list/detail), **Medium** (dynamic lists/limited charts), or **Large** (complex metrics/multiple charts). \* **Reflow Logic:** Dynamic cards (like Events) must have a defined maximum height. While desktop views allow internal scrolling, mobile cards SHALL NOT scroll internally to avoid "scroll-trapping." \#\#\#\#\# 5\. Interaction Rules and Micro-interactions Micro-interactions are the "satisfying clicks" that provide immediate functional feedback. A premium experience is achieved through **Subtlety, Smoothness, and Purpose** . \#\#\#\#\#\# Performance Mandate To maintain a 60fps standard, animations MUST be GPU-accelerated. \* **Allowed:** transform (scale, translate, rotate) and opacity. \* **Forbidden:** Properties that trigger layout recalculation, such as width, height, top, left, or margin. \#\#\#\#\#\# Timing and Easing We utilize **ease-out** easing to mimic real-world friction, ensuring the interface feels responsive and natural. | Element Type | Recommended Duration | Strategic Reason | | \------ | \------ | \------ | | **Small Buttons** | 150–200ms | Quick response feels snappy and responsive. | | **Color Shifts** | 150–200ms | Human perception detects color changes rapidly. | | **Icon Rotations** | 200–250ms | Prevents the movement from feeling jarring or "sticky." | | **Large Cards** | 250–300ms | Larger surface areas benefit from slower, more noticeable effects. | \#\#\#\#\# 6\.
